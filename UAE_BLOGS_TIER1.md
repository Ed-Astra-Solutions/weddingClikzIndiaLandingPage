**H1:** What Wedding Photography Really Costs in Dubai in 2026

---

Most "Dubai wedding photography cost" articles you'll read were written by someone who has never set foot in the Palazzo Versace ballroom. We're going to do this differently.

We shoot in Dubai most weekends. The numbers below are what couples actually pay — not the polished brochure rate, and not the headline figure a planner quotes to anchor you high. If you're trying to budget for your wedding, this is the page you wanted.

### The honest range — AED 8,500 to AED 1,40,000+

Wedding photography in Dubai in 2026 sits anywhere from **AED 8,500 for a small Nikah with a single photographer** to **north of AED 1,40,000 for a four-day Indian wedding with photo, film, drone, second shooters and a same-day edit**.

That's a 16x range, which isn't very useful on its own. So here's how we slice it.

### Tier 1 — Intimate weddings & Nikah-only coverage

- **Budget:** AED 8,500 – 18,000
- **What you get:** One senior photographer, 6–8 hours, ~400 edited images, online gallery, no album, no film
- **Typical venue:** Smaller hotel hall, beachfront ceremony, home Nikah
- **Where this works:** Civil weddings at the Dubai Courts, intimate Nikah at home, registry-only days. Not enough coverage for a full Indian wedding day.

### Tier 2 — Single-day weddings with film

- **Budget:** AED 22,000 – 45,000
- **What you get:** Photo + film team (2 people each side), 10–12 hours, ~800 edited images, a 3–4 minute cinematic highlight reel, online gallery, basic album
- **Typical venue:** Marriott Marquis Saeed Halls, JW Marriott Marquis, Address Sky View, Anantara The Palm
- **Where couples overpay:** Drone is usually quoted as "included" but only covers 30 minutes — extra hours billed at AED 1,500/hr. Ask.

### Tier 3 — Multi-day Indian weddings, luxury hotels

- **Budget:** AED 55,000 – 1,40,000+
- **What you get:** 4–6 person photo + film crew, 3–4 events (Haldi, Sangeet, Wedding, Reception), drone, gimbal, two cinematic films (highlight + traditional), full album, same-day teaser
- **Typical venue:** Palazzo Versace, Atlantis The Royal, Bvlgari, Madinat Jumeirah, Ritz-Carlton DIFC, Bulgari Resort
- **Reality check:** Indian weddings at Palazzo Versace and Atlantis start at around **AED 1,80,000 just for the venue** — about INR 40 lakh — before catering. Photography is rarely the line item that breaks the budget.

### Tier 4 — Private islands & published-spread coverage

- **Budget:** AED 1,50,000 – 4,00,000
- **What you get:** Editorial-grade direction, behind-the-scenes film, magazine submission, full creative team
- **Typical venue:** Bvlgari Resort private island, Anantara Sir Bani Yas Island, Six Senses Zighy Bay (just over the Oman border)
- **Why pay this:** You're not booking a photographer. You're booking a creative director who also happens to shoot. If you don't know why you'd want that, you don't need it.

### What the price actually buys

When we send a Dubai quote, the line items are almost always:

1. **Crew days** — biggest cost. A four-person team for two days is the bulk.
2. **Equipment redundancy** — two of every camera body, three lens kits, dual card slots on everything. We've never lost a frame in 11 years. This is why.
3. **Travel and logistics** — flights from Bengaluru, two nights' hotel, ground transport. We absorb this on bookings over AED 80k; smaller bookings see it itemised.
4. **Post-production** — 6 weeks of culling, editing, colour grading. This is silent labour but it's about 40% of the work.
5. **Deliverables** — film edit, album design, printing, shipping. Albums alone are AED 2,000–8,000 depending on size and binding.

### Where couples *quietly* overpay in Dubai

Three patterns we see, repeatedly:

- **Buying more crew than you need.** A 200-guest reception does not need six photographers. Two seniors and a film team do better work than six juniors.
- **Skipping the same-day edit and regretting it.** A 60-second teaser delivered the next morning is what your friends share. Twelve weeks later the final film lands and nobody reposts. AED 5,000 for the same-day edit is the highest-ROI line item on any package.
- **Booking the cheapest drone-only operator.** Dubai requires a DCAA permit for any drone work near hotels, beaches, or downtown. A studio quoting AED 800 for "drone shots at Atlantis" almost certainly does not have the permit. If something goes wrong, it's your wedding day that gets paused.

### FAQ

**Is wedding photography in Dubai more expensive than India?**
On a like-for-like crew, yes — about 1.6 to 1.9x. The difference is mostly travel, hotel, and the AED-denominated cost of equipment maintenance. If your photographer is flown from India, ask how those costs are split.

**How much should I budget for an Indian wedding in Dubai including everything?**
Most Indian weddings at four-star Dubai venues come in between AED 4,50,000 and AED 9,00,000 all-in (venue, catering, decor, photography, attire excluded). Photography is usually 8–12% of that.

**Do Dubai photographers charge in AED or INR?**
Both. If your photographer is India-based and you're paying in INR, get the exchange rate locked in writing. Currency drift between deposit and final payment has bitten couples for AED 4,000+ before.

**What's the deposit and refund structure?**
Industry standard in Dubai is 25–30% deposit, 50% one week before the wedding, balance on delivery. Refundable up to 60 days out, partial after that. Anything stricter — walk away.

**Are pre-wedding shoots usually included?**
No. They're a separate AED 6,000–18,000 depending on location and crew. We bundle them at 20% off if booked with full wedding coverage.

**Why do some quotes look 50% cheaper than yours?**
Three reasons, in order of frequency: (1) single-shooter coverage being marketed as "team coverage", (2) raw delivery instead of edited delivery, (3) no album or film. Compare line items, not totals.

**How early should we book?**
6–10 months. Peak Indian wedding season in Dubai is November–February and the good crews are gone by July. Last-minute slots exist but you give up venue/date flexibility.

Yes. Ask via the contact form. We'll send a redacted version of a Palazzo Versace wedding quote so you can see exactly how the numbers stack.

**H1:** Choosing a Wedding Videographer in Dubai — Past the Showreel

---

A wedding photographer captures the moment. A wedding videographer decides what the moment will *feel like* when you watch it five years from now.

Most couples in Dubai book the photographer first and treat the videographer as an afterthought. We'd argue you should do the opposite. Photos are a still life — beautiful, frame-able, but quiet. A film is what your kids will actually watch.

This guide is for couples deciding between styles, vendors, and budgets in Dubai in 2026.

### The three film styles you'll be quoted

**1. Documentary / traditional**
A linear edit of the day. Often 30–90 minutes. Ceremony, speeches, dances, exit. Useful if your families want every moment preserved. Boring to share.

**2. Cinematic highlight**
A 3–6 minute film cut to music, structured like a short film. This is the one your friends will rewatch. The good ones make the venue itself feel like a character — the Atlantis underwater suite, the Marina skyline at golden hour, the Madinat Jumeirah waterways at dusk.

**3. Editorial / story-driven**
8–15 minutes, with interview voiceovers from you and your partner laid over the visuals. Becoming the dominant style for 2026 weddings in Dubai. Expensive and slow to make — usually 12–14 weeks of post.

Most Dubai studios will quote you the cinematic highlight as standard. Ask what's *not* included before you sign.

### What separates a forgettable film from a great one

It's not the camera. Everyone shoots on a Sony FX3 or a Canon R5C. The differences are these:

- **Audio.** Most "cinematic" wedding films in Dubai have weak audio because the videographer used the on-camera mic for the ceremony. Ask if they're using lavalier mics on the groom and the celebrant. If they say "we'll handle it on the day", they're not.
- **The edit, not the shoot.** A 12-hour wedding day produces about 2 TB of footage. The film is made in the cutting room, not the venue. Ask to see the *full final film* from a recent wedding, not a 30-second Instagram reel.
- **Music licensing.** Dubai weddings filmed to commercial tracks get muted on Instagram and YouTube within hours. Good studios licence music through Musicbed, Artlist or Soundstripe. Bad ones use Spotify, then blame the algorithm.
- **Pacing.** Watch a sample film and notice when you check your phone. If you reached for it before the 90-second mark, the film isn't working.

### Drone work in Dubai — the part nobody talks about

If your quote mentions "aerial coverage" at Atlantis, Burj Al Arab, Palm Jumeirah, Burj Khalifa downtown, or any beachfront venue, ask one question: **"Do you hold a DCAA recreational or commercial drone permit?"**

The answer matters because:

- Drones over hotels, beaches and most of Downtown Dubai without a permit are illegal. Fines start at AED 20,000 and the operator can be deported.
- Several venues — Atlantis, Burj Al Arab, Madinat — require *additional* venue clearance even with a DCAA permit. This takes 7–14 days.
- If your videographer turns up without paperwork and gets stopped by security, you lose the aerial footage. There's no replay.

We hold the commercial DCAA permit and coordinate venue clearance as part of our booking process. Ask any vendor for theirs in writing before you pay a deposit.

Delivery timelines have stretched in 2026 because every senior editor in Dubai is booked. Realistic expectations:

- Same-day teaser (30–60 seconds): the next morning
- Cinematic highlight (4–6 minutes): 6–8 weeks
- Full ceremony cut (15–45 minutes): 10–12 weeks
- Editorial / interview-led film: 12–16 weeks

Anyone promising you a 4-week turnaround on a full edit is either skipping the colour grade or hasn't started the wedding season yet.

### FAQ

**Is a wedding film really worth the cost?**
You'll re-watch a good 5-minute wedding film maybe 20 times in the first year and a dozen times every anniversary after. Photos get framed; films get replayed. The cost per re-watch is genuinely low.

**Can one team shoot both photo and video well?**
Two separate teams, yes — one combined two-person team trying to do both, no. Avoid "photographer who also does video" quotes for anything bigger than a Nikah.

**Do I need 4K? 8K?**
4K is the standard. 8K is a marketing line — almost nobody watches it back at 8K and the file sizes are unreasonable. Don't pay extra for 8K.

We mic the celebrant in their language and add subtitles in the edit. Common in Dubai: Arabic + English, Tamil + English, Hindi + English.

On request, yes — to Vogue Weddings, Brides Today, Wedded Wonderland and the Indian-UAE wedding publications. Submission fees are on us; we keep the credit.

**H1:** Where to Shoot Your Pre-Wedding in Dubai (Without Looking Like Every Other Couple's Reel)

---

There are about 20 places in Dubai where 90% of pre-wedding shoots happen. We're going to walk you through the ones that are actually worth the money, with notes that no permit-counter clerk will tell you.

By the time you finish this, you'll know:
- Which locations need permits and which don't

- What to wear that won't blow out in the desert sun

### Iconic & instantly recognisable

**1. Burj Khalifa & Dubai Fountain**
The shot everyone wants. The shot that requires the most planning. Best window: 5:30–6:30 am when the fountain plaza is empty and the building sidelights are still on. After 9 am you're competing with 4,000 tourists. Permit required for tripods.

**2. Burj Al Arab from Sunset Beach**
The only public beach view of Burj Al Arab. Shoot 30 minutes before sunset. The sand is cleaner at the south end. No permit for handheld shoots.

**3. Museum of the Future**
The metallic exterior gives you mirror reflections that look like a sci-fi film. The 6 am light is unbeatable. Security stops shoots after 9 am.

### Beach & waterfront

**4. JBR Beach & Dubai Marina walk**
Dependable, well-lit, easy parking. Best at golden hour with the Marina skyline as backdrop. Get there 90 minutes before sunset so you have time to walk the promenade and the beach. No permit needed for couple shoots.

**5. Dubai Creek (Abra ride)**
Underrated. Hire a private abra (~AED 120) and shoot during the crossing. The old skyline of Deira behind you is unlike anything else in Dubai. Best at the 4 pm light.

**6. Bluewaters Island & Ain Dubai**
The world's tallest observation wheel makes a clean backdrop without needing to be inside. The Wharf side of Bluewaters has fewer tourists than the main walk.

### Old Dubai — the under-shot spots

**7. Al Seef**
Sandstone walls, wooden doors, low arches. Feels like a film set because, technically, it is — it was built in 2017 to evoke 1950s Dubai. Shoot 4–6 pm. Free entry, no permit for couple shoots.

**8. Al Fahidi Historical District (Bastakiya)**
The real old quarter. Narrow alleys, wind towers, courtyards. Slower pace. Bring a 35mm lens — you won't have room for a long.

**9. Madinat Jumeirah waterways**
A small abra through the canals with the Burj Al Arab in the background. Stunning at dusk. Resort permission required if you're not staying — AED 1,500 for a 2-hour slot.

### Desert & nature

**10. Al Qudra Lakes**
Our favourite desert location. Real lakes in the desert with flamingos in winter. Shoot at sunrise. Drive yourself; pickup vehicles get stuck.

**11. Al Qudra "Love Lake"**
Two heart-shaped lakes seen from above. You'll want a drone here — handheld coverage doesn't do it justice. Best at sunrise; by 8 am the wind picks up.

**12. Hatta**
90 minutes from Dubai. Mountain ranges, freshwater dams, rocky trails. The closest Dubai gets to a hill station. Worth a full-day trip for couples who want non-Dubai-looking shots.

**13. Liwa desert (technically Abu Dhabi)**
Three hours out. The biggest dunes in the UAE. Only worth it for a sunrise shoot. Requires 4x4 access — we book the vehicle and driver.

### Seasonal & special-permit

**14. Dubai Miracle Garden**
Open November–April only. Postcard-bright, slightly too much, perfect for couples who want colour. Get the 9 am slot before crowds; permits cost AED 1,000 for professional shoots.

**15. Atlantis The Palm — Royal Bridge Suite or beach**
Stay one night and the shoot is included on most Atlantis stays. The underwater suite shoot is unique but books out 4 months ahead.

### Permits and the reality of shooting in Dubai

The rule of thumb: handheld camera + couple shoot = no permit needed in 80% of public locations. The moment you add a tripod, lighting, drone, or a styled wardrobe change, you're in permit territory.

We handle all permits as part of our pre-wedding package — Dubai Film and TV Commission, individual venue clearances, and DCAA drone permits where needed. Average lead time: 7–10 working days.

### Timing — the hours that matter

| Location type | Best hour |
|---|---|
| Downtown / urban | 5:30 – 7 am |
| Beach / Marina | 4:30 pm – sunset |
| Old Dubai | 4 – 6 pm |
| Desert | Sunrise (sunset is too short) |
| Indoor / resort | 10 am – 12 pm, or 3 – 5 pm |

### What to wear

Three rules we give every couple:

1. **No pure white for the bride if you're shooting outdoors after 11 am** — it blows out and the dress detail disappears.
2. **Avoid prints with thin lines** — they moire on camera and ruin the edit.
3. **Bring two outfits per location** — a hero look and a relaxed second look. Adds 25% to your gallery for no extra shoot time.

**How long is a typical pre-wedding shoot in Dubai?**
4 to 8 hours, usually split across two locations. We rarely shoot more than three locations in a day — the changeover time kills your light.

AED 6,000–18,000 depending on locations, crew size, and outfit changes. Permits are typically AED 0–2,500 on top. We bundle the pre-wedding at 20% off if booked with full wedding coverage.

**H1:** Why More Couples Are Choosing Abu Dhabi for Their Pre-Wedding Shoot

---

Dubai's pre-wedding shoot locations are gorgeous, but every reel from every Dubai couple looks the same now. Burj Khalifa, JBR walk, Al Seef sandstone walls — we've all seen it.

Abu Dhabi is 90 minutes down the E11 and most of it photographs better than Dubai. It's also quieter, cheaper to permit, and the white architecture works for almost every wardrobe.

This is the locations list, in shooting order.

### The icon — Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

This is the location everyone wants and the one most couples get wrong.

**What works:** Exterior courtyards, the reflective pools at sunset, the colonnade arches just after the morning prayer ends. The marble is so clean it acts as a natural reflector — you don't need fill light.

**What doesn't work — and what will get you removed:**
- Posed couple photography *inside* the prayer halls. Not allowed, period.
- Bridal-style outfits with bare shoulders, exposed midriff, sheer fabric. The dress code is strictly modest.
- Tripods or external lighting without a permit. AED 2,500 fine on the spot.
- Photography during the five daily prayer times.

**The shot to actually take:** the early morning slot — 6:30 to 8:30 am. The light angles cleanly across the western colonnade. The crowd hasn't arrived. The marble is its truest white.

We coordinate the permit (DCT Abu Dhabi licence) and brief you on the dress code two weeks before. Brides typically wear a long-sleeved kurta or modest evening gown with a head covering; grooms in formalwear without ties. The shoot looks editorial, not touristy.

### Louvre Abu Dhabi (exterior)

The geometric dome that filters Saadiyat Island's light is one of the most photogenic structures in the Gulf. **Interior shoots are not permitted** for couples; you have to work the exterior.

The wins:
- Pre-opening hour shots from the entrance plaza with the dome's dappled light across the marble
- The waterfront edge at golden hour, with the dome silhouetted
- The bridge approach — clean, white, modern

Skip the interior queue. You'll spend AED 240 in entry fees and get shots a tripod-free guest could've taken.

### Qasr Al Watan (Presidential Palace)

White marble, gilded ceilings, and the kind of grand ballroom photographs that look like a film set. AED 65 entry; couple photography allowed in the public galleries and gardens.

This is where you shoot the "couture on the move" frames — bridal lehenga sweeping across the rotunda floor, the groom in formal sherwani against the carved walls. We shoot here mid-morning when the diffused dome light hits the East Gallery.

### Corniche & the Founder's Memorial

**Corniche Beach** — straightforward, dependable, golden-hour-friendly. Free. No permit needed for handheld couple shoots.

**Founder's Memorial** — an under-used location with a massive open square, a kinetic sculpture, and a view of the city skyline. Best at twilight when the memorial lights come on. Free entry.

### Saadiyat Beach

The cleanest white sand in the UAE, fewer crowds than Dubai's beaches, and the turtle-nesting season gives a soft conservation backdrop. Best in the late-afternoon window. Stay at Saadiyat Rotana or the St Regis Saadiyat to avoid the day-visitor permit drama.

### Yas Marina (and the F1 circuit perimeter)

Underused for pre-weddings. The W Hotel at Yas Marina has the rainbow facade; the marina walk has yacht-lined waterfront; the F1 circuit perimeter has industrial-modern frames that nobody else is shooting. Best at the 5 pm light.

### Liwa Desert — the dunes that are actually impressive

90 minutes south of Abu Dhabi city. The Tal Mireb dune is 300m high — the biggest in the UAE. Liwa makes Dubai's Al Qudra look like a sandbox.

Worth a separate sunrise trip with a 4x4 transfer. We bundle Liwa as an overnight add-on at AED 7,500 including the vehicle, driver, and a 4-hour shoot window across sunrise.

### Qasr Al Hosn

The oldest stone building in Abu Dhabi. Heritage-tile walls, narrow doorways, a working museum. The grounds shoot beautifully with traditional Indian and Emirati wardrobes. Modest entry fee, no permit drama.

### The under-shot location: Al Ain (90 minutes east)

If you have a full day, drive to Al Ain. The Jebel Hafeet mountain road, the date palm oases, Al Jahili Fort, and the camel market all photograph like nothing else in the UAE. Camera bag in the boot, lunch at the Mercure on Jebel Hafeet, sunset on the mountain road. This is the shoot none of your friends will have.

The default rule: more modest than Dubai. Specifically:

- For the Mosque: full-sleeve, ankle-length, head covering for the bride
- For Qasr Al Watan: formal evening or traditional wear
- For beaches: anything goes
- For Louvre and Corniche: smart casual to formal

Bring at least two outfits even if it's a half-day shoot. The light changes locations every 90 minutes and a wardrobe shift gives you 35% more gallery variety.

### FAQ

**Can I shoot inside the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?**
Only photographs respectful of the prayer halls. No posed couple shoots inside, no flash, no tripods without permit. Exterior and courtyard shoots are where you'll get the best frames anyway.

**Do I need a separate permit for each Abu Dhabi location?**
For most public spaces, no. For the Mosque, Qasr Al Watan, and Louvre exterior with tripod/lights, yes. We handle them.

**What's the dress code I should plan for?**
Modest at religious sites and palaces. Standard at beaches and the Corniche. Dubai-style outfits work at Yas, Louvre exterior, and Founder's Memorial.

**Can we drive to Abu Dhabi from Dubai in one day for the shoot?**
Yes, and many couples do. Leave Dubai by 6 am, shoot the Mosque sunrise window, brunch, second location, golden-hour shoot, back by 9 pm. We can arrange the driver and the day plan.

The flagship. Gold-leaf ceilings, the longest hotel ballroom in the country, and a 1,400-room footprint that means crew logistics matter as much as creative direction.

A Venetian-style waterfront resort with a 3,000m² ballroom that handles 500 guests cleanly.

Beach-resort luxury, calmer feel than the Corniche venues. Tall ceilings in the ballroom, but the real magic is the beachfront.

**What we love:** the beach ceremony arch setup. The sand at Saadiyat is the whitest in the UAE; the ocean light is forgiving even at noon. Sunset ceremonies here are unbeatable.

**What's hard:** the walk from bridal suite to ballroom passes through public hotel corridors. We position a photographer at the lift and one at the suite door to manage the moment.

**Best for:** couples wanting a beach wedding with proper banquet quality. Indian weddings under 250 guests.

### Grand Hyatt Abu Dhabi (Emirates Pearl)

The Al Bateen Ballroom at 1,000 m² is one of the largest in Abu Dhabi. Excellent for large Indian and Arab weddings.

**What we love:** the pre-function space — 550 m² of clean light for cocktail-hour candid shoots. The VIP lounge for parent and grandparent portraits.

**What's hard:** the ballroom is windowless. Plan early for the lighting brief — we usually request the venue's lighting be set to 3200K tungsten warm for the dinner and switch to cooler for the sangeet.

**Best for:** large weddings (300+ guests), Indian baraat processions, ceremonies with multiple stage setups.

### Saadiyat Rotana Resort & Villas

The most under-rated venue in Abu Dhabi for our money. Lower price than St Regis or Emirates Palace, with arguably better beach access for ceremony shoots.

**What we love:** the villa pool deck for intimate Mehendi or Haldi morning coverage. The boardwalk to the beach is a 400m runway for the couple session.

**What's hard:** less prestigious than the icon venues. If "venue brand" is part of your wedding pitch, this isn't the one.

**Best for:** couples prioritising photographs over hotel-name prestige. 100–250 guests.

Bahrain is the Gulf's quietest wedding destination — and for the right couple, the best one.

### Why couples are choosing Bahrain in 2026

Three reasons we keep hearing:

1. **Cost per guest is lower than Dubai.** Four Seasons Bahrain Bay quotes around 30% lower per guest than the equivalent Four Seasons in Dubai (DIFC). For a 250-guest Indian wedding, that's a meaningful number.
2. **Less crowded venue calendar.** Ritz-Carlton Bahrain and Four Seasons routinely have dates available 3–4 months out — unheard of for equivalent Dubai venues in peak season.
3. **Quieter destination for guests.** Smaller airports, shorter immigration queues, more relaxed pace. Your guests arrive less stressed.

### The venues — what to shoot at

**Ritz-Carlton Bahrain**
The pioneer luxury venue on the island. Beach access, palatial ballroom, private island for sunset shoots. The Lagoon Pool deck at golden hour is genuinely one of the most photogenic locations in the Gulf.

- Best for: 150–400 guest Indian weddings, beach ceremonies
- Photo strength: outdoor variety. Six distinct backdrops within the resort
- What to plan for: long walk from bridal suite to outdoor ceremony — schedule 25 minutes for the bride's transit

**Four Seasons Hotel Bahrain Bay**
The newer flagship. The Al Bahrain Ballroom has 7-metre ceilings and the kind of natural light through the lobby that most ballrooms only get in marketing renders.

- Best for: 200–500 guests, premium Indian and Arab weddings
- Photo strength: the lobby, the bay-facing terrace, the rooftop at sunset
- What to plan for: book the bridal suite on the same floor as the ballroom if available — the standard suites are five floors up

**Sofitel Bahrain Zallaq Thalassa**
On the southwest coast, more isolated, palm-filled grounds, and the resort's private "Sofi Island" for off-grid photography.

- Best for: smaller, more intimate weddings (80–200 guests), beach destination feel
- Photo strength: the private island ferry ride, the palm-grove ceremony aisle
- What to plan for: distance from Manama — your guests should plan an overnight stay

**Exhibition World Bahrain**
Modern, large-scale, banquet-style venue. Less photographic charm than the resorts but unbeatable for very large Indian weddings (500+ guests) at lower cost per head.

- Best for: 400–1,200 guest weddings on a tighter budget per guest
- Photo strength: the Grand Hall scale. Wide reception shots look genuinely grand
- What to plan for: the venue is windowless. Lean heavily on indoor decor and lighting

**Smaller / boutique:**
- **The Merchant House** in Manama souq — for very small weddings and post-wedding shoots
- **Wyndham Grand Manama** — solid mid-range alternative
- **Diplomat Radisson Blu** — convenient for civil and Nikah-only ceremonies

### Bahraini etiquette your wedding photographer should know

This is where most external photographers stumble:

- **Gender-segregated weddings are common.** If your wedding is segregated, you need separate male and female photographer/videographer teams. Booking one couple-team is not enough.
- **Some families ask for no photography of female guests.** Confirm before the wedding day. We carry a written brief from the family to give to the team.
- **Ramadan and Eid timing.** Wedding bookings shift dramatically around Ramadan. If your date is within two weeks of Ramadan, your shot list needs to account for venue availability, evening-only ceremonies, and crew fasting considerations.
- **Friday weddings vs Saturday weddings.** Friday is the holy day; ceremonies are usually evening. Saturday is the most popular wedding day in Bahrain, much like UAE.
